Why a mountain-base city sees more than burst pipes

Arcadia's elevation is 482 feet, and the city runs along the foot of the San Gabriel Mountains. Sloped ground pushes surface water toward foundations, garages and lower rooms. That means a heavy storm can bring water in from outside in the same season a supply line fails inside, and those are two different problems.

Before you assume anything about outside water, look up your address on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center at msc.fema.gov. Then ask LA County Public Works or the city about drainage near your street. We don't guess at flood exposure from a street name, and what the map says decides whether flood insurance belongs in your conversation at all.

The California Department of Insurance notes that standard homeowners policies typically exclude flood, mudslide and debris flow. Flood coverage comes through the National Flood Insurance Program and generally takes effect 30 days after purchase, so buying it after the water arrives is too late for that event. Read your own policy and ask your insurer before repairs begin.

When water is spreading in Arcadia

Start with the source. Close the shutoff at the fixture, or the main valve if the source is unclear. If it's runoff coming in from outside, no valve will stop it, so move belongings to higher ground and keep everyone out of the wet rooms.

Switch off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and easy to reach. If water has touched outlets, the panel or appliances, stay out. Phone the utility or 911 about any electrical or gas concern before you call us. Storm runoff and sewage count as Category 3 under ANSI/IICRC S500, meaning grossly contaminated, so nobody should wade through it.

Dispatch runs 24/7, and the first call is mostly questions about where the water entered, how many rooms are wet and what the floors are made of. Crews then bring truck-mounted or portable extraction units, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers as the job needs. EPA guidance says drying wet materials within 24 to 48 hours usually prevents mold, which is why a night call is worth making.

Flood work first, then mitigation, then rebuild

For Arcadia, the question we hear most is how to handle water that came in from outside. Flood damage restoration covers extraction of standing water, removal of wet materials that can't be saved, and structural drying, using moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras to find what's still wet behind the paint.

If the cause was inside, a pipe or an appliance supply line, the job leans on water mitigation, the early stage of stopping the damage from growing. Mitigation removes water and sets drying equipment. It doesn't fix the pipe, and it doesn't replace drywall.

Once the structure reads dry, water damage repair covers the rebuild, from drywall and baseboards to flooring. Scope and price are explained before any work starts, and we don't state numbers on a web page because every house is different.

Arcadia's city water system and your bill

The City of Arcadia says it is the sole provider of water and sewer services to more than 50,000 residents, run through Public Works Services at 240 West Huntington Drive. Their service request line is (626) 254-2720. Golden State Water also lists portions of Arcadia, so check your bill to see who actually supplies your address.

That matters in an emergency. A break in the supply line on the street side of your meter is a utility call, while a leak after the meter is yours to stop and ours to dry. Knowing your supplier saves minutes when you're deciding who to phone first.

Before we arrive: what to do while water is still spreading

Cut off what is feeding the water

Turn off the fixture valve or the main shutoff. Runoff from outside can't be valved off, so focus on keeping it out of more rooms.

Power down wet zones only if the panel is dry

If you can reach the panel without stepping in water, switch off the wet circuits. If not, leave it and call the utility.

Raise what you can carry

Get electronics, papers, rugs and small furniture onto beds, counters or shelves above the waterline.

Keep people and pets clear

Storm runoff and drain backups carry contamination, so nobody should wade through standing water.
