Most homes in San Marino were built between 1920 and 1950, on lots that are larger than almost anywhere else in the San Gabriel Valley. That mix of age and size changes how water damage is handled. A pipe failure upstairs in an older home can reach several rooms before anyone notices, and a thorough dry-out takes more equipment than a typical house.

This page explains how those two facts, age and size, shape our work in San Marino, and how our 24/7 line gets started on a call.

Homes from 1920 to 1950 and what water does to them

San Marino has 4,482 housing units, and most were built between 1920 and 1950. Older construction often has plaster or thicker wall assemblies, original hardwood floors and trim milled to profiles you can't buy off a shelf. Water behind those materials dries slowly, so moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras matter more than a visual check.

We can't promise what's inside any particular wall without opening it, and we don't assume the age of your pipes. What we can say is that older finishes deserve a slower, more careful plan. We lay out that plan in writing before work starts, so you can decide what to save and what to replace.

Design review, matching finishes and the city

San Marino applies strict design review and zoning, and the city splits into seven zones by minimum lot size. That means a repair that changes the look of your house may need more than a contractor's say-so. Before you pick new trim, paint or flooring, ask the City of San Marino what applies to your lot.

Water damage repair on a house like this often means matching existing baseboards, plaster texture or hardwood species. Matching takes planning, and some profiles have to be custom milled. Permit rules vary by job and city, so ask before you commit to a scope.

Big lots, big footprints and longer drying

Zoning here sets the smallest lot at about 4,500 square feet, and many lots top 30,000. Large footprints mean long runs of pipe, more rooms on a single leak path and longer hoses from a truck-mounted extractor to the wet area. A single break can affect a wing, not just a room.

That's where water mitigation earns its place. Crews place air movers and LGR dehumidifiers room by room, check readings every day, and move equipment as areas dry. Classes under ANSI/IICRC S500 run from 1 to 4 depending on how much material is wet, and big houses often land in the higher classes.

When water is spreading in San Marino

Water travels in a big house, so move fast on the shutoff. If you can't tell which valve feeds the leak, close the main. Then pull rugs and small furniture off the floor and open closet doors so you can see how far it has gone.

Shut off wet circuits only when the box is on dry ground and nothing is dripping near it. If water is at outlets or the panel, stay away and call the utility or 911 before you call us. Anything from a toilet or drain is Category 3 and should be left to crews wearing the right gear.

Dispatch is 24/7. We ask how many floors are wet, what the floors are made of and whether anything valuable is in the path, then bring extraction and drying equipment to match. The EPA's advice is a 24 to 48 hour window for drying before mold becomes likely.

Lacy Park, Huntington Drive and the places crews use as reference points

Lacy Park covers 30 acres with two walking loops, six tennis courts and a Rose Arbor. The Centennial Clock stands at Huntington Drive and San Marino Avenue, across from City Hall. Name whichever one is closest and dispatch will know the area at once.

The Huntington Library, Art Museum and Botanical Gardens spreads over about 120 acres. Near large properties, access gates and long driveways are common, so tell us about gate codes or a caretaker who can let crews in. Your neighbors to the south are covered on our San Gabriel page.

Before we arrive: what to do while water is still spreading

Close the valve that feeds it

In a large house the right shutoff may be far away, so go to the main if the fixture valve isn't obvious.

Power off wet wings, if the panel is dry

Large homes often have several panels. Touch one only if you can reach it without standing in water.

Protect the fine pieces

Rugs, artwork, books and upholstered furniture come off wet floors and walls first.

Hold people back from dirty water

Anything from a drain, toilet or outside is contaminated, so keep family and pets out.

Call and describe the footprint

Tell the dispatcher at (626) 879-0276 how many rooms and floors are wet.

San Marino history that helps on a service call

San Marino was incorporated on April 25, 1913, and 12,513 people lived there in 2020 across 3.77 square miles. A small city with large homes means a single street can have very different house sizes. We always ask about square footage and floors so the equipment on the truck fits the job.

Landmarks like the Governor Stoneman Adobe, El Molino Viejo and the Edwin Hubble House, a National Historic Landmark, show that the city values its older buildings. If your house is old, tell us on the call, because careful removal and documentation matter for any structure with historic features.
