About Temple City Water Damage Pros

A local 24/7 dispatch line for water damage and mold in the San Gabriel Valley.

☎ Call (626) 879-0276

Temple City Water Damage Pros is a 24/7 water damage line for Temple City, California and the nearby towns of Arcadia, San Gabriel, Rosemead, San Marino and El Monte. Calls are answered around the clock and dispatched to licensed and insured restoration professionals.

Our dispatch address is 9123 Las Tunas Drive, Temple City, CA 91780. Temple City was laid out by Walter P. Temple in 1923 and incorporated in 1960, and we built this site around the way people in this part of the San Gabriel Valley actually describe water problems: a ceiling stain, a failed water heater, a washer hose, a smell after a leak.

What happens when you call

A dispatcher asks where the water is coming from, which rooms are wet and whether the water looks clean or contaminated. While help is being arranged, you get plain steps to take, such as closing the main water valve, lifting valuables and keeping power off in wet areas unless the panel is dry and safe to reach.

On site, the work starts with finding the moisture. Meters and thermal imaging show how far water travelled behind walls and under floors, and that map becomes the written scope. The scope and price are explained before work starts, so you decide with the facts in front of you.

Drying is finished when the readings say so, not when a calendar says so. The published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes how professionals measure and document that, and you are entitled to see the final readings before any rebuilding starts. If anything on the site or on the phone sounds vague, ask for specifics.

What we do and what we do not

We handle water mitigation, cleanup, repair, emergency extraction, flood damage and mold remediation. We do not replace plumbing or roofing, and we do not waterproof foundations. When the source of the water needs a plumber, roofer or drainage contractor, we say so.

Restoration is also not a promise about the future. Drying and repair return a home toward its pre-damage condition, but they cannot stop a new leak. If a supply line, water heater or roof is still failing, that needs its own repair.

How we talk about standards and credentials

ANSI/IICRC S500 and S520 are the published standards for water damage restoration and mold remediation, and we encourage you to ask any company whether its crews work to them. We describe our crews as licensed and insured, and we encourage you to verify any contractor with the Contractors State License Board at cslb.ca.gov before you sign.

California requires a licensed contractor for work worth $1,000 or more in labor and materials. A written contract should state the total price, the scope, the timeline and the contractor license number, and the down payment is capped at the lesser of $1,000 or 10 percent. These are the county consumer protection rules, and they apply to every company you consider, including us.

Insurance

We document damage with photos, moisture readings and a written scope. Coverage depends on your policy, so check it or ask your insurer. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, gradual leaks often are not, and flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage.

Why this site is built the way it is

Every page on this site is written for a person with a real problem and a short amount of time. That is why the pages explain what to do in the first hour, what to ask a contractor and what an honest limit looks like, instead of listing claims you cannot check.

You will not find star ratings, invented testimonials or years-in-business figures here, because none of those can be verified from a web page. What you will find are public standards, public agencies and plain explanations of how a job is scoped, so you can judge any company, including this one, on facts you can confirm yourself.

The guides in the blog follow the same rule. Each one cites public sources such as the California Department of Insurance, the Contractors State License Board and the EPA, and each ends with the local details that change the advice in Temple City.
