What black mold means as a label, and how to vet a company before you sign anything.

Search for black mold removal companies and you will find plenty of confident claims. Choosing well takes a few specific checks: what the company says it will do, how it will contain the work, whether its license is real, and who verifies the result. This guide gives you a checklist you can run in one phone call and one website visit.

It starts with the label itself. Black mold is a popular phrase, not a diagnosis, and treating it as one leads people to overpay or panic. Nothing here is medical advice, and health questions belong with a physician.

What black mold means, and what it does not

Black mold is a casual name for dark mold growth. Color does not identify a species. Many different molds appear dark, and some dark stains are not mold at all. Only sampling and laboratory analysis by a qualified party can say what a specific growth is.

For the homeowner, the practical point is simple. Mold of any color needs moisture, and the response follows the same logic. Find and fix the water, contain the affected area, remove what cannot be cleaned, and dry the structure. Be wary of any company that treats a dark patch as a special emergency and skips the moisture question.

A checklist for choosing a company

Run these checks before you accept a visit, a scope or a deposit.

  • Written scope: it should list rooms, materials removed, cleaning methods, containment and disposal.
  • S520 question: ask whether the crew works to ANSI/IICRC S520, the published standard for professional mold remediation.
  • License: work worth the state's licensing threshold needs a contractor licensed by the Contractors State License Board. Most mold jobs cross that threshold, so verify the license at cslb.ca.gov.
  • Insurance: ask for proof of general liability insurance, and check workers' compensation on the CSLB site.
  • Containment plan: ask how they will keep spores out of the rest of the house, including barriers, negative air and HEPA filtration.
  • Moisture plan: ask how they find the source, and how they confirm the structure is dry.

The common restoration license classes on the county's list are B (General Building), C-21 (Demolition) and C-61 (Limited Specialty). A class on its own does not mean the work is good, but a missing or mismatched class is a reason to ask more questions.

Questions to ask on the first call

Good answers are specific and calm. Weak answers are vague, rushed or built around fear. This table shows what to listen for.

You do not need to be an expert. You only need to notice whether the answers come with details.

Red flags to walk away from

Pressure and vagueness are the two patterns to watch. A company confident in its work does not need either.

  • A diagnosis or health prediction based on color or smell. That is outside what a contractor can know.
  • A promise that mold will never return. Mold needs moisture, and no company controls your future leaks.
  • A request for a large deposit up front. The county guide caps a down payment at the lesser of a fixed dollar limit or 10 percent of the contract price.
  • No written contract, or a contract with no CSLB license number.
  • A price offered before anyone has seen the space, or a scope that changes as soon as you hesitate.
  • A company that tests and certifies its own work as the only proof.

Testing, clearance and independence

Many homeowners, especially those selling or refinancing, want a document showing the work is complete. That is clearance testing, and it carries the most weight when done by a party with no stake in the cleanup. Ask the contractor whether they support an independent tester's access and schedule.

Testing before work can also help define scope, but it is optional and separate from remediation. Contract and payment protections

The LA County Department of Consumer and Business Affairs publishes the rules worth knowing. A written contract should state the total price, payment schedule, scope, change-order terms, timeline, permits, the contractor's CSLB license number and the cancellation policy.

Use lien waivers with every payment, and do not make the final payment until work is complete and you have lien releases. A home improvement contract can generally be cancelled within three business days, five if you are 65 or older, and seven if your home was damaged in a major disaster.
