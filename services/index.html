Water damage is a sequence, not a single job. Standing water comes out first, then wet materials dry, debris is cleaned out, and repairs finish the work. Each page below explains one stage and who it is for, and the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the yardstick for water work while S520 covers mold.

If water is still moving, start with emergency water removal. If it has soaked into walls and floors, water mitigation covers the drying. For the main page on the whole process, see water damage restoration in Temple City.

How the services fit together

A typical job moves through the pages in order. Extraction removes standing water, mitigation dries what soaked in, cleanup removes what cannot be saved and sanitizes what can, and repair rebuilds. Flood damage and mold remediation follow the same logic but add safety steps, such as protective equipment for sewage or containment barriers for mold.

Scope and price are explained before work starts, and the price depends on the water category, the affected area, the materials involved, the drying time and the rebuild scope. If you are unsure which stage you are in, call and describe what you see. We will tell you honestly whether you need flood damage restoration, a mold assessment or just a plumber.

None of these services replaces a plumber, a roofer or a waterproofing contractor. Fixing the source of the water is always the first step, and we point you to the right trade when that is the case.
